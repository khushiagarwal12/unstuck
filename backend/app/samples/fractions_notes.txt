Fractions: Study Notes for Grade 6-7

A fraction names a part of a whole, or a part of a group. The bottom number is the denominator. It tells us how many equal parts the whole is cut into. The top number is the numerator. It tells us how many of those equal parts we are talking about. In the fraction 3/4, the whole is cut into 4 equal parts and we take 3 of them. The parts must be equal. If a pizza is cut into one big slice and three small slices, each slice is not one quarter.

A larger denominator means smaller pieces. One eighth of a cake is smaller than one fourth of the same cake, because the cake was cut into more pieces. When the numerators are the same, the fraction with the smaller denominator is larger.

Equivalent Fractions

Equivalent fractions name the same amount using different numbers. The fractions 1/2, 2/4 and 4/8 are equivalent. To make an equivalent fraction, multiply the numerator and the denominator by the same non-zero number. Multiplying both by 3 turns 2/5 into 6/15. The size of each piece changes, and the number of pieces changes, but the amount stays the same.

To simplify a fraction, divide the numerator and the denominator by a common factor. Dividing both parts of 12/18 by 6 gives 2/3. A fraction is in simplest form when the only common factor of the numerator and denominator is 1. Adding the same number to the top and bottom does not give an equivalent fraction. Adding 2 to both parts of 1/2 gives 3/4, which is a different amount.

Comparing Fractions

To compare two fractions with the same denominator, compare the numerators. The fraction 5/9 is greater than 4/9 because five pieces of the same size are more than four pieces. To compare two fractions with the same numerator, compare the denominators. The fraction 3/5 is greater than 3/8 because fifths are larger than eighths.

When both parts differ, rewrite the fractions with a common denominator, then compare the numerators. To compare 2/3 and 3/4, rewrite them as 8/12 and 9/12. Since 9/12 is greater than 8/12, the fraction 3/4 is greater than 2/3. Another method is to compare each fraction with one half or with one whole. The fraction 4/9 is less than one half, and 5/8 is more than one half.

Adding and Subtracting Fractions

When fractions have the same denominator, add or subtract the numerators and keep the denominator. The sum of 2/7 and 3/7 is 5/7. The denominator does not change, because the size of each piece stays the same. We are counting pieces of the same size, so we do not add the denominators.

When the denominators are different, first rewrite the fractions so they share a common denominator. To add 1/3 and 1/4, use 12 as the common denominator. Then 1/3 is 4/12 and 1/4 is 3/12, and the sum is 7/12. Adding the numerators and the denominators separately, to get 2/7, is wrong. The answer 2/7 is smaller than 1/3, but adding a positive amount to 1/3 must give a number larger than 1/3.

Fractions on the Number Line

A fraction is a number, and it has a place on the number line. To place 3/4, mark the distance from 0 to 1, divide it into 4 equal parts, and count 3 parts from zero. Each part is 1/4 long. The marks must be equally spaced, and the whole segment from 0 to 1 is the unit.

Fractions can be greater than 1. The fraction 5/4 sits one quarter of a unit past 1. Two fractions that are equivalent land on the same point. The points for 1/2, 2/4 and 3/6 are all the same point. Reading a number line correctly means counting the spaces between marks, not the marks themselves.

Improper Fractions and Mixed Numbers

An improper fraction has a numerator that is greater than or equal to the denominator, such as 7/4. A mixed number shows the same amount as a whole number plus a fraction, such as 1 3/4. To change 7/4 into a mixed number, divide 7 by 4. The quotient 1 is the whole number part, and the remainder 3 is the new numerator, over the same denominator 4.

To change a mixed number into an improper fraction, multiply the whole number by the denominator and add the numerator. For 2 1/3, compute 2 times 3, which is 6, and add 1 to get 7. The improper fraction is 7/3. The mixed number 2 1/3 means 2 plus 1/3. It does not mean 2 times 1/3.

Multiplying Fractions

To multiply two fractions, multiply the numerators together and multiply the denominators together. The product of 2/3 and 4/5 is 8/15. Unlike addition, you do not need a common denominator to multiply fractions.

Multiplying by a fraction less than 1 makes the result smaller than the number you started with. One half of 3/4 is 3/8, which is smaller than 3/4. We can think of multiplying 1/2 by 3/4 as taking half of three quarters. An area model helps: shade 3/4 of a square, then shade half of that shaded part. Multiplication does not always make a number bigger when fractions are involved.
